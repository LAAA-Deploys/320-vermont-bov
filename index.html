Executive Summary

Transit Site
About 120 feet from the Vermont/Beverly Metro B Line station
The site's southwest corner sits roughly 120 feet from the station entrance at Vermont Avenue and Beverly Boulevard. That puts it inside a Transit Priority Area with no required parking under AB 2097 and in the strongest incentive tiers of the City's housing programs.

320 N Vermont Avenue and 321 N Juanita Avenue are two contiguous parcels totaling 20,517 SF, both owned by Hubbard College of Administration. The Vermont parcel (12,999 SF, C2-1) is improved with the College's four-story, 25,100 SF building, built in 1962 and renovated in 2001. The Juanita parcel (7,518 SF, R4-1) is unimproved. The two lots share a 50-foot rear lot line and form an L-shaped through-lot with frontage on both streets.

Both parcels are in Subarea C (Community Center) of the Vermont/Western Station Neighborhood Area Plan, which allows R4 residential and commercial uses on either lot. Eight development pathways apply, from about 51 units by right, to about 110 to 115 units under the City's Mixed Income Incentive Program, and a 100% affordable building of roughly 130 to 180 units. Across Vermont, an affordable developer bought 315 N Vermont in January 2025 at $322 per land SF and has filed a 113-unit, 12-story building. We recommend a list price of $5,000,000 ($243.70 per land SF).

Location and Designations

1
At the station. The Vermont/Beverly B Line station is about 120 feet from the site's southwest corner. Wilshire/Vermont (B and D Lines) is about one mile south, and the 101 Freeway is two blocks north. The block scores 91 for walkability (Walk Score).
2
HUD Qualified Census Tract. Tract 1927 is a QCT for 2026 and 2027, and ZIP 90004 is a Small Area Difficult Development Area on HUD's 2027 list. A tax-credit project here can increase its eligible basis by up to 30%, which raises the credit equity the same project can attract.
3
An affordable housing corridor. Across Juanita, a 72-unit permanent supportive housing building received its Certificate of Occupancy in October 2026. Across Vermont, Holos Communities has filed a 113-unit affordable building at 315 N Vermont.

Key Takeaways

1
The corner has already priced above $320 per SF. The two sales on the subject's own corner closed at $327.49 and $322.06 per land SF, about $80 above the two-mile average of $241.44.
2
Affordable capital is buying here. The sale across Vermont went to an affordable developer now filing 113 units, and a 72-unit supportive housing building just opened across Juanita. QCT status supports the tax-credit math.
3
Station proximity drives density. At about 120 feet from the B Line, the site sits in the T-3 incentive area, where a mixed-income project roughly doubles the by-right unit count with no required parking.
4
The building gives a second buyer pool. The 25,100 SF building can convert under the Adaptive Reuse Program or serve an institutional user, which most of the comparables did not offer.
5
Priced under Measure ULA. At $5,000,000 the sale stays below the City's $5,400,000 ULA threshold, so neither party pays the 4% transfer tax that applies to the full price above it.

Below the $5,400,000 threshold

At $243.70 per land SF the list price sits at the two-mile average of $241.44 per SF and well below the two sales on the subject's own corner at $322 to $327 per SF. That leaves room for the cost of removing or converting the existing building and the site's L shape, and gives buyers a clear discount to the corner's recent trades. Measured the way buyers underwrite it, $5,000,000 is about $44,600 per unit on a mixed-income project and about $32,300 per unit on a 100% affordable building, below the $35,398 per unit an affordable sponsor paid across the street.

The price also stays under Measure ULA. Above $5,400,000 the City's transfer tax is 4% of the full price, so an offer between $5,400,001 and $5,625,000 would net the seller less than a $5,400,000 sale. If competition pushes the price up, offers should be countered either to $5,400,000 or above $5,625,000.
